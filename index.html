Read the narration transcript

Creating character animation takes more than a text prompt. Artists sketch routes, place poses, direct body parts, and preserve existing motion. AnimateCanvas represents position and rotation cues on a shared motion canvas. A shared flow model coordinates the motion. Cue imputation preserves the specified canvas values throughout generation.

A dense route specifies where to travel. A local wrist cue controls one body part without specifying the rest of the motion. Sparse foot targets and root-heading constraints leave the transitions open. AnimateCanvas connects them with a continuous, coordinated gait. Position and rotation cues can be combined across body parts and time within a single motion. A few pose and trajectory cues shape the jump, while the model completes its timing and whole-body coordination.

An existing motion can also serve as editing context. Here, the original motion provides editing context, while language changes the gesture to wave the right hand. The edited motion follows the instruction while maintaining a coherent surrounding action. Compatible cues share the same motion canvas, so one generator can complete motion under their combined constraints.

Local targets guide the strike, while the model coordinates stance, timing, and recovery. This is cue-conditioned implicit motion planning: connecting the partial states prescribed by cues into one coherent full-body trajectory. Here, we provide the hoop position at the dunk time. AnimateCanvas plans the full motion around it. Thank you for watching.

Goodbye.

Method at a glance

Kinematic cues on one shared motion canvas.

The motion canvas stores cue values and a generation mask across joints and time. One shared flow-matching model generates motion conditioned on this canvas, with optional language and input motion. Cue imputation restores the specified canvas values after each sampling step.
